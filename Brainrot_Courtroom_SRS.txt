1. OVERVIEW

1.1 Product Summary
Brainrot Courtroom is a fast-round Roblox social deception game where one or more hidden troublemakers cause absurd disasters, plant misleading evidence, and try to avoid conviction while the rest of the lobby investigates, debates, and votes.

The game combines:
- short social deduction rounds
- absurd meme-style presentation
- fake and incomplete evidence
- high player interaction
- repeatable rounds with fast resets
- cosmetic progression and unlocks

The core fantasy is:
Something ridiculous happened. Everyone is panicking. Figure out who caused it before the round ends.

1.2 Product Type
Multiplayer Roblox experience.

1.3 Vision
Build a highly replayable social deception game with low onboarding friction, strong clip potential, and a chaotic courtroom identity that feels instantly watchable.

1.4 Core Differentiator
Unlike a traditional deduction game focused on realism, Brainrot Courtroom emphasizes absurd disasters, questionable evidence, false accusations, and comedic courtroom presentation while preserving a clear and competitive round structure.

2. PROBLEM STATEMENT

Many Roblox social games struggle with one or more of the following:
- rounds take too long to start
- roles are confusing
- players get bored if they die or are removed early
- social deduction games feel too serious or too slow
- meme games are funny once but lack replayable structure
- party games are hard to spectate or understand quickly

Players want:
- a game that is easy to understand in under a minute
- fast, dramatic rounds
- funny accusations and betrayals
- enough structure for skill and replayability
- a strong group-play experience

Brainrot Courtroom solves this by combining a simple social deception loop with absurd incidents, accessible evidence systems, and courtroom-style debate and voting.

3. GOALS

3.1 Primary Goals
- Create a highly replayable short-session multiplayer game
- Make the core loop understandable in under 60 seconds
- Encourage social interaction, accusation, bluffing, and baiting
- Generate funny and shareable moments naturally
- Support both public lobbies and friend-group play

3.2 Secondary Goals
- Provide strong cosmetic and progression incentives
- Make spectating and watching a round entertaining
- Enable quick live-ops additions through new disasters, modifiers, and evidence types
- Keep content scope manageable for a small development team

3.3 Non-Goals
- realistic legal simulation
- deep narrative progression
- realistic detective mechanics
- large open-world exploration
- complicated combat systems
- heavy inventory management

4. TARGET USERS

4.1 Primary Users
A. Casual Roblox Players
Players who want quick, funny, social multiplayer rounds.

B. Friend Groups
Small and medium groups looking for a party game with betrayal and voice/text chaos.

C. Content-Friendly Players
Players drawn to games that create clip-worthy reactions, accusations, and dramatic reveals.

4.2 Secondary Users
- streamers and short-form creators
- younger audiences who enjoy meme-chaos presentation
- players who like deduction games but want less seriousness

5. GAME PILLARS

5.1 Easy to Enter
The premise and controls should make sense almost immediately.

5.2 Social Chaos
The best moments should come from accusation, bluffing, and panic.

5.3 Fast Rounds
A player should be able to finish multiple full rounds in a short session.

5.4 Funny but Structured
The game should feel absurd, but the rules must still be fair enough to sustain competition.

5.5 Spectator Value
Even players not actively controlling the outcome should still be entertained by reveals, debates, and reactions.

6. CORE GAME LOOP

6.1 High-Level Loop
1. Players join lobby
2. Roles are assigned secretly
3. A disaster occurs or is triggered
4. Players gather clues, react, and discuss
5. Court session begins
6. Players accuse and defend
7. Vote occurs
8. Verdict is revealed
9. Winning side is rewarded
10. Round resets with new roles and modifiers

6.2 Session Structure
- lobby and matchmaking
- repeated short rounds
- cosmetic and progression rewards between rounds

7. ROUND STRUCTURE

7.1 Phase 1: Role Assignment
Each player receives a hidden role.

Example roles:
- Saboteur
- Civilian
- Witness
- Chaos Lawyer
- Forger
- Judge Modifier role in special modes

7.2 Phase 2: Incident
A ridiculous event occurs.

Examples:
- giant screaming head falls from sky
- lunch room explodes in confetti and fire
- vault opens and cursed objects spill out
- giant fake evidence printer spawns nonsense reports
- all lights cut out for 10 seconds
- floor becomes slippery while one room locks

The incident creates the reason for the trial.

7.3 Phase 3: Investigation
Players move around a compact map, inspect clue points, observe behavior, and interact with witnesses or evidence systems.

7.4 Phase 4: Courtroom Debate
All players are moved to a courtroom or debate scene.
Players can:
- accuse others
- defend themselves
- present evidence
- interrupt through limited systems
- bait reactions

7.5 Phase 5: Vote
Players vote on who they believe caused the event.
Depending on the mode, they may vote:
- one suspect
- multiple suspects
- guilty or not guilty
- whether to trust a witness

7.6 Phase 6: Reveal and Rewards
The true role alignment is revealed.
Winning side gets rewards.
Funny recap text or highlight moments are shown.

8. ROLES

8.1 Civilian
Goal:
Identify and convict the correct saboteur or sabotage team.

Abilities:
- inspect evidence
- discuss and vote
- use role-neutral interactions

8.2 Saboteur
Goal:
Cause the disaster and avoid conviction.

Abilities:
- trigger or influence incidents
- manipulate evidence in limited ways
- lie during debate
- exploit panic and confusion

8.3 Witness
Goal:
Help civilians by receiving partial but imperfect information.

Abilities:
- receive one clue that is useful but incomplete
- may be wrong, ambiguous, or manipulable

8.4 Forger
Goal:
Assist deception by creating or altering one piece of evidence.

Abilities:
- plant misleading clue
- generate fake report
- frame another player under limited rules

8.5 Chaos Lawyer
Goal:
Create confusion and disrupt certainty.
Can be independent in some modes or aligned to saboteurs in others.

Abilities:
- add doubt
- distort debate flow
- earn rewards for causing wrong conviction or split vote, depending on rules

8.6 Special Event Roles
Future updates may add:
- Judge
- Bailiff
- Double Agent
- Silent Witness
- Janitor
- Meme Oracle

9. MAP DESIGN REQUIREMENTS

9.1 MVP Map Goals
- compact enough for fast investigation
- easy to learn
- multiple sightline choke points
- at least one hidden or isolated space
- easy transition into courtroom

9.2 MVP Map Theme Options
- cursed school
- bizarre office
- meme courtroom complex
- disaster lab
- chaotic shopping center

9.3 Map Requirements
- 6 to 14 player support
- clear clue points
- obvious player routes
- minimal downtime between spaces
- strong visual read for spectators

10. EVIDENCE SYSTEM

10.1 Design Philosophy
Evidence should be helpful but never perfectly decisive in every round.

10.2 Evidence Types
- witness statements
- camera snapshots
- item traces
- room access logs
- audio clues
- fake documents
- environmental anomalies
- partial timeline fragments

10.3 Evidence Rules
- some clues are reliable
- some are ambiguous
- some can be forged
- no single clue should guarantee the answer every round
- evidence must create discussion, not replace it

10.4 MVP Evidence
The MVP should include:
- 3 reliable clue types
- 2 ambiguous clue types
- 1 forgable clue type

11. GAME MODES

11.1 Core Mode
One saboteur, one incident, standard courtroom vote.

11.2 Chaos Mode
More fake evidence, faster rounds, more absurd modifiers.

11.3 Team Sabotage Mode
Two hidden saboteurs coordinate.

11.4 Hardcore Mode
Reduced evidence clarity, higher penalties for wrong conviction.

11.5 Limited-Time Modes
Seasonal or event-based rule changes.

12. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

12.1 Lobby and Match Flow
The system shall:
- allow players to join a waiting lobby
- begin rounds when minimum player count is met
- assign roles secretly
- return players to post-round state cleanly
- support repeat rounds without leaving server

12.2 Role Assignment
The system shall:
- assign roles fairly based on lobby size
- conceal roles from unauthorized players
- scale role composition with player count
- support special-role toggles for future modes

12.3 Incident System
The system shall:
- trigger a round-start incident
- generate context for investigation
- support multiple incident templates
- allow certain roles to influence incident outcomes when applicable

12.4 Investigation System
The system shall:
- let players move through the map
- surface clue interactables
- track who interacted with certain systems when needed
- reveal clues according to role permissions and round logic

12.5 Debate and Court System
The system shall:
- transition players into a courtroom or debate scene
- display suspect lineup or player identifiers clearly
- allow accusation and defense messaging
- support timed speaking or text windows in structured mode
- support voting at the end of the debate phase

12.6 Voting System
The system shall:
- allow each eligible player to cast a vote
- prevent duplicate vote submissions
- resolve ties using defined game rules
- reveal results clearly
- support mode-specific vote logic later

12.7 Reveal System
The system shall:
- reveal role alignment after verdict
- display whether the correct player was convicted
- declare winning side
- distribute round rewards accordingly

12.8 Rewards and Progression
The system shall:
- reward participation
- reward winning alignment
- reward streaks or role mastery later
- unlock cosmetics, titles, emotes, or courtroom themes

13. NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

13.1 Performance
- rounds should start reliably on typical Roblox-compatible devices
- map complexity should remain appropriate for mobile and low-end users
- debate and voting transitions should be low-latency and readable

13.2 Reliability
- role assignment must remain secret and accurate
- vote outcomes must be deterministic and verifiable by server logic
- round reset must not leak prior-round state

13.3 Usability
- first-time players should understand the objective within one round
- role instructions must be short and clear
- evidence UI must be readable and quick to parse

13.4 Safety and Moderation
- communication systems should account for Roblox moderation constraints
- quick chat or structured accusation prompts should exist if free chat is limited
- grief opportunities should be controlled where possible

14. UX REQUIREMENTS

14.1 New Player Onboarding
The game shall:
- show a short tutorial panel before or during the first round
- explain role secrecy, evidence, accusation, and voting
- avoid long mandatory tutorials

14.2 HUD Requirements
The HUD shall show:
- round phase
- time remaining
- current objective
- role-specific guidance
- evidence count or available clue actions
- vote prompt during courtroom phase

14.3 Spectator Experience
Eliminated or inactive players shall still be able to:
- watch debates
- watch reveals
- see funny recap cards
- remain engaged without influencing active play unfairly

16. MONETIZATION STRATEGY

16.1 Design Principles
Monetization should:
- not decide guilt or innocence directly
- not create pay-to-win role advantages
- prioritize cosmetics, expression, and convenience
- avoid predatory randomization patterns

16.2 Monetization Options
- cosmetic outfits
- emotes
- courtroom podium skins
- banner/title customization
- premium victory effects
- battle pass or season pass
- VIP private server perks
- extra role-variant cosmetics, not stronger roles

16.3 Monetization Non-Goals
- no paid evidence advantages
- no paid vote multipliers
- no role purchase that guarantees sabotage power in competitive matchmaking

20. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

20.1 MVP Round Flow
- a full round can start, progress through all phases, and end cleanly
- players receive hidden roles correctly
- at least one incident occurs and creates an investigation context
- debate and voting happen reliably
- reveal correctly identifies the winning side

20.2 Evidence
- players can find and interpret at least several clue types
- evidence influences discussion without hard-solving every round
- at least one evidence type can be misleading or forged

20.3 Replayability
- players can complete multiple rounds in a session without excessive downtime
- different role assignments and incidents meaningfully change round feel

21. BALANCE TARGETS

21.1 Round Length
Target average:
- 5 to 8 minutes per round

21.2 Lobby Size
Target:
- 6 to 12 players ideal
- support 4 player minimum with simplified rules if needed
- support up to 14 later if performance and balance allow

21.3 Role Balance
Early target:
- civilian side win rate near 50 to 60 percent
- saboteur side win rate near 40 to 50 percent
Exact balance will be adjusted through live data.

23. RISKS

23.1 Design Risks
- rounds may feel repetitive if clue variety is too low
- pure chaos may overpower fair deduction
- social games can die fast if lobbies feel empty

23.2 Technical Risks
- role secrecy leaks or vote bugs would damage trust immediately
- too much map complexity may hurt performance
- moderation constraints may reduce freeform debate quality

23.3 Product Risks
- meme branding may attract attention but not retention
- a younger audience may struggle with deduction depth if onboarding is weak
- the game may need enough players to feel alive

24. MITIGATIONS

- keep first version simple and readable
- bias toward fair structure, then layer chaos on top
- use clear phases and timers
- build strong quick chat or accusation UI if needed
- add bot fill or minimum-player fallback modes later if required
- ship content updates regularly to maintain novelty

25. SUCCESS METRICS

25.1 Early Success
- high first-round completion
- strong repeat rounds per session
- clear evidence of friend-group replayability
- players understanding the premise without external explanation

25.2 Medium-Term Success
- increasing retention
- strong clip and share potential
- healthy cosmetic conversion without pay-to-win complaints
- growing direct traffic from creator content or social posting

26. RECOMMENDED MVP CUT

The leanest strong MVP is:
- one compact map
- one saboteur
- civilians
- witness
- forger
- 5 incidents
- 6 clue types
- one courtroom debate phase
- one vote system
- round rewards
- basic cosmetics
- short onboarding

That is enough to prove the thesis:
a meme-chaos courtroom game can be funny, fast, and replayable if the deduction loop is clear.
